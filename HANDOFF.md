The remaining teacher-policy decision is material and must not be guessed. Historical read-only reconstruction established that legacy `old` means a literal count of **separate earlier-grade-origin questions** in a 32-example batch, `preview` means separate future-grade questions, and `distill` is a KL coefficient against a frozen pre-attempt parent on retention rows. Champion-voted current questions, failed-exam replay and embedded prerequisite constituents are different semantics. Mapping any of those onto `old`, `preview` or `distill` would change the learned policy's meaning and reward distribution. A diagnostic-conditioned option selector therefore needs its own versioned, source-attested policy/reward admission and explicit user confirmation before it may affect live human training.
