The current [Grade A repair and backend evidence](../evidence/grades/GRADE_A_REPAIR_AND_BACKEND_20261015.md) documents a third independent source-record oracle, balanced membership cardinality, a 216-item frozen *candidate* manifest, exact 72-item assessment scoring, and full-student disposable Apple GPU parity. **Do not treat the historical v1 source-probe digest or this earlier assertion as a current training-admission receipt.**

## Observed source foundation

The audited historical FP32 checkpoint has an append-only graph containing 222 semantic tokens. Source: `bilqis_micro/graph.py` and `bilqis_logic/kernel.py` in the preserved v16 worktree (historical source only). The archived compiled 29 `G##` headings are **capabilities**, not the new school grades. The original lesson dispatcher and source-fixed assessment must not be carried forward as the new grade hierarchy without restructuring.

The foundation available from the original explicit semantic graph and independently implemented oracles includes:

- Boolean literals `cogal.value.true` and `cogal.value.false`;
- Boolean variables `cogal.variable.P` and `cogal.variable.Q`;
- typed individual entities `micro.entity.A` through `micro.entity.E`;
- relation arguments and direction via `micro.role.source` and `micro.role.target`;
- equality root `micro.compare.EQ`;
- direct relation roots including `micro.relation.PARENT` and `micro.relation.LINK`;
- numeric identities and readout tokens (historical arithmetic modules and `school.number.0` onward) requiring an explicit new narrow-range teaching/assessment contract before use;
- later Boolean operators, inverse relations, quantifiers, proof rules, set operations and function composition that **must not** be introduced as composed Grade A tests.

## A-grade partition candidates for source-based review

These are **candidate capability neighborhoods, not an executable eight-capability Grade A**. Several may need to be grouped or split after comparing difficulty and actual teachable permutations. Their existence in the token vocabulary is not proof that the model understands them or that independent evaluation fixtures exist.

Not all nine necessarily belong in A. Domain, difficulty, independence and prerequisites require actual lesson/exam coverage analysis before promoting any proposed partition. A real first-grade course will choose about 6–9 justified foundational capabilities and include bounded nonbinary answers where semantically appropriate, with binary answers in each capability.

## Required admission tests

- Every capability references a specific existing semantic root/stem/scope and an independently checked oracle, not merely an English name.
- Grade A has 6–9 similarly difficult, genuinely distinct teachable capabilities. No synthetic filler to reach a quota.
- Every assessed root has appeared in lessons; no previously unseen source morphology or abstract target.
- No composed predicate in Grade A requires a constituent that can only be mastered in a later grade.
- Every capability's assessment covers its appropriate bounded answer families, including at least one binary family.
- Train/test seeds are disjoint by semantic AST/world identity, not just by phrasing or incidental Boolean clauses.
- The actual per-grade same-checkpoint criterion and whole-school final evaluation are fresh versioned rubrics; the historic 1,150-item exam remains provenance, not the graduation contract.

**Status:** `GRADE_A_SOURCE_VALIDATION_HOLD`. This document is a source-guided shortlist, not user-approved capability allocation or evidence that MLX neural training may start.

## Source-backed six-capability draft, 2026-10-15

The private `bilqis_mlx.grade_a_probe` now generates six single-predicate proposals: `A.IDENTITY`, `A.PARENT`, `A.LINK`, `A.MEMBER`, `A.EDGE` and `A.MAPS`. They test entity equality, directed parent facts, symmetric links, set membership, graph edges and direct function mappings. Each is a flat Boolean lookup with no quantified, temporal, inverse, subset or composed query. The source of each predicate is the existing `bilqis_micro.kernel` or `bilqis_development.kernel`, verified by five pinned SHA-256 source hashes.

The reproducible audit yields 24 instructional and 12 assessment questions per predicate (216 total). Each partition has balanced TRUE/FALSE labels; both old independently implemented symbolic oracles agree. No train/exam input identity repeats, including across capabilities. Each capability teaches every token and world size it later examines. **Both three- and four-entity worlds appear in training and testing.** The tests vary mathematical relations or queried operands, not unrelated Boolean decorations.

A read-only check against the preserved complete step-116760 FP32 checkpoint confirms the first **113** source-token IDs are exactly the initial 113 entries of the saved **222**-token vocabulary. The additional 109 tokens stay unchanged. The full model graph and original file SHA are pinned in `docs/provenance/GRADE_A_SOURCE_PROBE_V1.json`.

**Decision still open:** Six direct predicates are a viable source-tested draft, but their comparable difficulty and ordering still require evaluation. In particular, `MEMBER`, `EDGE` and `MAPS` draw on more developed typed world structures than `IDENTITY`, `PARENT` and `LINK`. The proposal is **not** a training-admitted Grade A, and no real-student score or mastery receipt has been recorded. The old model may already recognize the roots; that does not establish new-grade mastery. Numeric 0–3 answer readouts remain reserved for a grade whose numeral prerequisites and independent assessment contract are taught first. The workbook's provisional grade labels and New Ithkuil research bridges are not imported as new exam answers.
